## What Qualifies as a Dental Emergency?

A dental emergency is a problem that requires prompt assessment because of significant pain, infection, injury or a risk to oral health. Some conditions need same-day dental attention, while others require immediate hospital care.

Common dental emergencies include:

- **Severe toothache:** Persistent or intense pain may indicate deep decay, inflammation or an infection inside the tooth.
- **Broken or cracked teeth:** Damage caused by falls, accidents or biting hard foods may expose sensitive inner tooth structures.
- **Dental abscesses:** A pocket of pus caused by bacterial infection can produce pain, swelling and an unpleasant taste.
- **Knocked-out teeth:** An adult tooth knocked out by an injury needs immediate dental attention because timely treatment may improve the chance of saving it.
- **Lost fillings or crowns:** A damaged restoration can expose the underlying tooth and cause sensitivity or discomfort.

Not every dental problem requires an extraction. A dentist will examine the affected area and recommend treatment according to the diagnosis, the extent of the damage and the possibility of preserving the natural tooth.

## Which Symptoms Require Immediate Dental Attention?

Certain symptoms should not be ignored, even if the pain temporarily improves. Persistent tooth pain, increasing gum swelling, bleeding after an injury and pain when biting are reasons to contact a dentist promptly.

Fever accompanied by dental pain or facial swelling may indicate an infection that needs urgent assessment. A dental abscess will not reliably resolve on its own, and delaying treatment can allow the infection to spread.

## What Treatments May Be Available During an Urgent Dental Visit?

The appropriate treatment depends on the underlying problem. During an urgent appointment, a dentist will ask about your symptoms, review your medical history and examine the affected teeth and gums. Dental X-rays may be needed to identify damage or infection that is not visible during the examination.

Treatment options may include a filling for a damaged tooth, a crown to restore a weakened tooth, root canal treatment for an infected tooth or drainage of an abscess when indicated. Extraction may be considered when the tooth is too damaged to restore safely.

The dentist may also recommend suitable pain relief and explain how to care for the affected area after treatment. If specialist care is needed, a referral may be appropriate.

Early assessment can help identify the cause of the symptoms and establish a treatment plan before the condition becomes more complicated.

## How Can You Manage Dental Pain Before Your Appointment?

While waiting for professional care, simple measures may help reduce discomfort. They should not replace a dental examination.

- Rinse gently with warm salt water if appropriate and comfortable.
- Apply a cold compress to the outside of the cheek to help manage swelling.
- Eat soft foods and avoid chewing on the affected side.
- Take over-the-counter pain medication only as directed and if it is safe for you.
- Avoid placing aspirin directly on a tooth or gum, as it can injure the surrounding tissue.

Do not attempt to drain an abscess, pull out a tooth or repair a broken tooth yourself. These actions can cause further injury or delay necessary treatment.

If the pain becomes severe, swelling increases or you develop a fever, seek urgent professional advice rather than relying on home remedies.

## How Can You Reduce the Risk of Future Dental Emergencies?

Although not every dental emergency can be prevented, consistent oral care can reduce the likelihood of problems caused by untreated decay and gum disease.

Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste, clean between your teeth every day and attend regular dental check-ups. Treating small cavities before they progress can help prevent more extensive damage. People who play contact sports should consider a properly fitted mouthguard, while avoiding chewing ice and other very hard objects can reduce the risk of chipped or fractured teeth.

Do not postpone an appointment simply because tooth pain disappears. Some dental conditions can continue to develop even when symptoms temporarily improve.
